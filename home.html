The problem is bigger than a calendar

One timetable has to satisfy many people and scarce resources.

A workable schedule has to respect hard constraints—then make the best possible trade-offs among preferences, demand and campus realities.

01 / PEOPLE

Instructor availability

A teacher cannot be in two rooms at once. Teaching load, availability and preferred time patterns all shape feasible assignments.

02 / SPACE

Rooms, capacity and equipment

A room must be free, large enough, and suitable for the class—especially for labs or specialist teaching spaces.

03 / STUDENTS

Course and cohort clashes

Required courses that overlap—or leave too little time to reach the next room—can make a timetable unusable for students.

04 / CHANGE

Disruptions ripple outward

When a room or instructor becomes unavailable, a quick move can create new conflicts. Coordinators need to see the impact before notifying people.

How EVOYOU works

Rules do the checking. People make accountable decisions.

Not an “AI makes a timetable” black box. The product combines explicit scheduling constraints, search for feasible alternatives, and a coordinator-controlled approval workflow.

STEP 01

Set the academic constraints

Define courses, sections, instructor availability, room capacities, room features and the student groups or course requests that must be accommodated.

STEP 02

Build and validate the schedule

Check teacher, room and student conflicts against hard rules; then compare preferences such as balanced teaching loads and usable student days.

STEP 03

Compare a recovery shortlist

See a low-disruption recommendation beside distinct day/room alternatives, the configured hard checks, and the affected class cohort.

STEP 04

Decide before the schedule changes

A coordinator chooses and reviews one option. The current workspace applies only after confirmation; it does not publish to a campus system.

EVOYOU’s product bet: a short recovery brief with a recommended move, meaningful alternatives, visible constraint checks and an explicit coordinator decision—not a black-box “AI changed your timetable.” The combination is a hypothesis to validate with schedulers, not a claim that no competitor has similar tools.View the recovery workspace →

Research behind the workflow

Scheduling is a mature operations problem.

Student-demand scheduling, pathway planning, change workflows, section optimization, analytics and SIS connections already appear in established platforms. EVOYOU does not claim those capabilities are new. Its focused product bet is a coordinator-ready disruption brief: compare distinct feasible moves, show which hard checks filtered alternatives, make cohort impact visible, and let a person choose before anything changes. This is a testable workflow focus—not a claim of global novelty.
